## Operational playbook

### 1. Brief and evidence

In the existing brief/objective and research notes, record:

- Who is watching, what practical confusion they have, and one thing they
  should understand afterwards.
- Whether the idea belongs to movement, mechanism, myth, progression or
  comparison. Broad motivation needs a concrete fitness connection.
- What can be pictured with approved capabilities. An unsupported movement
  is a research/rig task, not a renamed existing exercise.
- Which claims need qualified input early, and which depictions need G3.
  Do not contact experts or represent their approval without authorisation.

Rank candidate ideas by **need, verifiable evidence, drawable proof and scope
fit**. A popular title, a publisher's audience-size claim or an affiliate
product is not scientific evidence or proof of demand for Sinew.

### 2. Story and script

Use the existing story-writer, not a parallel writing pipeline:

1. Draft a beat outline and a one-sentence viewer outcome.
2. For each causal statement, name the changed picture and the dossier claim
   that supports it. Keep claim IDs available through storyboard and review.
3. After the body draft, compare three original hook options against its actual
   payoff. Reject stronger promises rather than stretching the evidence.
4. Read the script aloud. Remove filler, repetitive sentence scaffolds and
   prose that only works on a page; keep terminology and uncertainty intact.
5. Keep source cards adjacent to the claim they support. Do not narrate a
   bibliography when a concise source line will do.
6. Put any commercial material in an explicitly separate, authorised segment;
   never use a product pitch as the scientific resolution.

Suggested **Sinew adaptations**, not alleged Yellow Dude shot sequences:

| Story need | Original beat pattern | Existing mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explain a movement | Whole movement -> relevant phase -> one causal cue -> whole movement again | Exercise library, tracked annotation, phase map |
| Compare | Name one variable -> matched camera/scale/phase -> show difference -> explain scope | Existing `stage.views` split views, annotations, evidence badge |
| Progression | State entry assumptions -> current task -> easier alternative -> next-step criterion | Existing registered exercises + original cards; research any new movement first |
| Myth | Fair version of belief -> testable prediction -> evidence -> qualified takeaway | Existing myth-busting pattern, charts, source card |

Do not force every film through every pattern. Use the requested form and
existing duration/pacing rules; no new timing numbers are inferred from YD.

### 3. Picture and movement

- One visual explanation at a time: character/action first, the relevant
  anatomy or path second, concise label/source third.
- Use viewpoint to answer a question, not to show that a camera can orbit.
- In a comparison, hold camera, scale and movement phase constant unless their
  change is explicitly the subject. Label geometry versus pose changes.
- Keep activation, anatomy, measurements and illustrative numbers distinct.
- Check silhouette and anatomical joins before lighting/detail. Test neutral,
  side/rear and relevant moving poses; a one-mesh or numeric pass is not approval.
- Where a pose is held for explanation, develop an annotation, path or cutaway.
  Do not leave narration on a frozen canvas.
- Test title/pose readability at intended phone size. A dramatic cover must
  still make the same bounded promise as the film.

### 4. Review and publishing

Before polishing, answer: can the rough sequence communicate the promised
distinction without music, sound effects or decorative motion? If not, route
it back to story/visual planning.

At final review, trace the chain **claim -> spoken clause -> drawing ->
source/uncertainty -> takeaway**. A correct sentence paired with a misleading
picture is a failure. Human G1/G3 are still required; AI review is not a signature.

Plan at least two original cover concepts during story/design work, then choose
after a small-screen check. Use our source/credit records honestly. Where useful,
derive a recap/checklist and a separately written short from the same verified
lesson. No storefront, outreach, affiliate agreement or publishing is authorised
by this reference study.

## What not to onboard

- The character, yellow/black branded identity, logo, costumes, exact compositions,
  drawings, scripts, voice identity, footage, product UI or third-party
  pop-culture characters appearing in covers.
- After Effects, Frame.io, Epidemic Sound, a human-actor pipeline, a 4K minimum
  or a five-week schedule merely because YD-03 reports them. These are not new
  dependencies or Motion Lab requirements.
- A blanket rule that wellness topics cannot work, or an assumed viral/retention
  formula. We do not have their analytics or our own validation for that claim.
- Provocation that shames bodies/beginners. Humour can target familiar training
  frustrations, never injury, disability or evidence uncertainty.
- Training prescriptions, diagnosis from symptoms, EMG percentages or simple
  binary physiological stories from marketing/editorial pages. For example,
  YD-05's general intensity/volume framing, YD-06's size-versus-strength
  taxonomy and YD-07's symptom-to-muscle explanation are **not adopted as facts**.
  Recheck primary literature, populations, conditions and causality through
  our research protocol before any such claim enters a film.

### Validation and next evidence

On the next suitable film, record which principles were used in the existing
brief/review notes, run the established gates, and ask reviewers what the
picture communicated before explaining it to them. Record aggregate findings,
not personal or patient data. Review whether the intended distinction, source
and caveat survived; do not infer success from views or a passing build alone.

For motion-specific learning: obtain permitted reference media, keep it outside
the repo, run the existing reference-corpus ingest/analyse workflow, inspect
timestamped evidence, and use Earshot before judging audio. Only then add
measured technique records or claims about edit timing/sound.
