# Fitness storytelling: explainers, myth-busting, exercise tutorials

The story-writer rulebook (`.claude/skills/story-writer/SKILL.md`) and the form skill (`short-form`, `mid-form`, `long-form`) govern every film. This file adds what is specific to fitness.

## The Sinew shape in one line

**Movement -> inside the body -> evidence -> back to the movement.** Open on Tibi doing something the viewer does, turn the body to glass to show the mechanism, put the grade and source on screen, and close on the same movement now understood.

## Pattern 1: mechanism explainer ("How your muscles actually grow")

| Beat | Fitness content | Kit |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Tibi mid-movement plus the surprising claim or the myth | `titleCard`, `callout`, `mythStamp`, exercise reps |
| Context | Only the anatomy the mechanism needs, named once, with scale | x-ray, `zoomLadder`, lower thirds |
| Explanation | 3-5 steps of one mechanism, each a changed picture | `sarcomere`, `sci.flow`, `evidenceBadge` |
| Demonstration | The mechanism running over time, or the key study's result | `sci.chart` (measured points), `crossSection.grow`, study card |
| Conclusion | Back to the movement; a three-part takeaway; one practical line | `endCard`, `sting` |

## Pattern 2: myth-busting ("Does soreness mean growth?")

1. State the myth in its strongest, most honest form (who believes it and why it feels true).
2. The test: what would we see if it were true? (one prediction, pictured).
3. The evidence: the best study or review, with design and n on screen, graded.
4. What is actually going on: the mechanism, inside Tibi.
5. What to do instead, framed as findings, not prescriptions, plus the general-education line.
Rules: one myth per film, the myth stamp once, no mocking the viewer ("you've heard", never "you're wrong").

## Pattern 3: exercise tutorial ("The squat, muscle by muscle")

1. The movement whole, in side view, at real tempo (2 reps).
2. Phases: hold the relevant pose while its annotation develops. Show surface effort for the *primary* muscles from `K.library.exercises[id].muscles`, or a labelled anatomy window with its distinct colour language; do not conflate the two. Say "mainly" (never "only"). Narration never continues over a frozen canvas.
3. Joint actions with angle arcs (labelled illustrative) and the bar path.
4. One or two common errors, using the kit's error poses with a coaching face. Show the fix, never pain.
5. A cue the viewer can use, then the movement again with the cue.
Camera: side view for sagittal mechanics; mention when a front view would show knee tracking (the 2D rig cannot).

## Per-form adaptation (adapt, never trim)

- **Short (20-60 s, portrait):** one claim from the long story's strongest moment. Hook in 3 s with Tibi moving, one mechanism picture, one number with its source, the payoff echoing the hook word. Tibi at scale 1.25 or more, centred high; captions in the mid lane for the hook. A short is a new film built around a single insight, not the first minute of a long one.
- **Mid (60-180 s):** one mechanism plus one demonstration; a re-hook about every 45-60 s in landscape ("Here's the catch"), every 35-45 s in portrait.
- **Long (5-12 min, landscape):** chapters with descriptive titles (`G.chapterCard`), one mechanism each, at most three new terms per chapter. Narrative spine: a question the viewer has ("Why do runners lift?"), each chapter answering part of it, a "what we don't know yet" chapter beat, and a resolution that returns to Tibi on the track or in the gym. Use several visual systems across chapters (exercise, anatomy, biomechanics, charts) so it never feels like one diagram for ten minutes. Sources slate before the end sting.

## Writing rules for the Sinew narrator

- Second person and concrete: "your biceps", "one hard session", "about a day or two".
- Numbers as researched, with who was measured: "in one study of ten young men".
- Grade words map to badges: established -> "we know"; strong -> "the evidence is strong"; moderate -> "the best explanation so far" or "studies suggest"; preliminary -> "early work hints"; hypothesis -> "one idea is"; unsupported -> "the evidence doesn't back this".
- Never: "scientifically proven", "the secret", "hack", "toxins", "burn fat from your belly", "isolate", "activate X%".
- Every film that suggests an action ends with the general-education line on screen and in the description.

## Hooks that fit the brand

Myth flip ("Lifting doesn't grow your muscles by tearing them."), concrete question ("Why do runners get faster from slow runs?"), small change, big effect ("Lighter weights can build as much muscle, if the last reps are hard."), human scale ("Your calves load several times your body weight with every running step."). Every number in a hook must be in the dossier, and every hook passes the Truth, Pull and Clear gates.

## Reference-informed authoring pass

Source distinctions and adoption limits: [Yellow Dude study](yellow-dude-study.md).
These are Sinew rules, not copied scripts or an assertion about video retention.

- State the viewer's concrete problem and one promised understanding before
  listing anatomy. Keep training/running context, not generic motivation.
- Carry dossier claim IDs into the planned drawing and review notes. Ask for
  qualified input on scope early; technical approval stays pending until the
  appropriate human review, never inferred from an attractive storyboard.
- Finalise three original hook options after the evidence/body draft exists.
  The selected promise must be paid off by what the viewer actually sees.
- Read aloud for spoken language: remove filler and repetitive scaffolds,
  without deleting qualifiers or changing technical meaning.
- A comparison changes one declared variable at a matched camera, scale and
  movement phase. A progression identifies entry assumptions and a sourced
  easier alternative; never invent readiness criteria or rename an unsupported rig motion.
- Review the unpolished animatic before decorative sound or finish. Follow
  Motion Lab's approved-story/measured-audio ordering, not another studio's schedule.
- Sketch original cover concepts during story/design work. Keep any authorised
  commercial segment separate from the scientific takeaway.
