# China's 2015 market crash: research notes

## Scope

This short explains the 2015 Shanghai equity sell-off as a leverage-amplified feedback mechanism, not as a single-cause diagnosis. Figures are rounded for mobile storytelling and sourced in [`research.json`](./research.json).

## Verified narrative

The Shanghai Composite rose by about 150% from mid-2014 to its June 2015 peak, then fell about 32% by 8 July. The exact drivers were plural: policy signals, retail speculation, margin finance, valuation and changing risk appetite all mattered. The animation makes one constrained claim: if investors are financed by collateral-sensitive borrowing, falling prices can automatically create more sell pressure.

For a simplified levered position with starting equity \(E_0\), leverage \(L\), and price decline \(\delta\), equity is illustrated as \(E_1=E_0(1-L\delta)\). This is a teaching model, not a reconstruction of every Chinese broker rule. It visually explains why a thin equity layer can disappear faster than the underlying asset price.

## Visual grammar

The curve conveys the rally and drawdown; the stack conveys collateral hierarchy; the order funnel conveys the scarcity of bids; the circular loop conveys reflexivity. These are original combinations of registered reference techniques, not reproductions of any source material.
